Why I'm Running

I've loved this city ever since my family moved here in 2014. But never have I loved it so much as I did this past winter. When ICE came for our neighbors, nobody asked whether it was their job to do something. We just started doing whatever we could — anything that might help in some small way. We organized an active network of rapid responders, and we did our absolute best to make sure that nobody in our city could be brutalized or disappeared by those thugs.

When we faced escalation after escalation, and the murder of two citizens who were fighting for justice the same way we were, we didn't back down. We gritted our teeth and kept up the good work, knowing every day very well could be our last — praying every night that no ICE agent would meet the same violence they were meeting us with, because we were so afraid of what more unlawful and unhinged evils the government would do to us if we'd only give them the chance.

The fight isn't over. ICE still operates in our city, just a little more quietly now. We are still here, doing all we can to keep our community safe. When I talk to my neighbors, when I ask them about their experience during the metro surge, I am often met with a fear to speak up — because they feel they might be met with retaliation from the government.

The people who will risk everything to use what little power they have to protect this community are the ones we should be electing as our leaders. I believe in the power of human beings working together to overcome evil. Strength in Community is more than just a slogan to me. It is what we found this year when we came together to give it our all against a threat too big for any one of us.

Compared with that work, the job of a councilmember is simple. I promise to listen to the needs of this community, and to give them a voice in city hall.

Priorities

  • Protect this community. I sincerely hope that what happened this last winter will never happen here again. But hope isn't a plan, and we can't afford to be so unprepared. Now let's be realistic — the power of a city councilmember isn't a lot. But what I can promise is that I will work tirelessly to make sure this city is ready — the policies, the networks, the preparedness — so the next time the federal government comes after our neighbors, the city of Shakopee will stand with you.
    • Flock cameras need to go.

      There are several directly outside my neighborhood, and over a dozen around Shakopee. They are an unbelievable security and privacy violation, and it is unconscionable for our city to do anything other than completely terminate the contract and remove all offending cameras.

  • Make life more affordable. We need to secure the affordability of Shakopee and insulate our community from the turbulence of this administration's disastrous economic impact on working families. There are lots of ways we can build a sustainable future — and almost all of them are at the local level. We can invest in local businesses. When we invite big companies to our city, we can make sure we're choosing companies that treat their employees with dignity and respect and that offer a fair wage. But we can also invest directly into the households that live here. I want to make it easier for households to get micro-generators, such as rooftop solar panels, so your electricity usage goes from a bill you pay to a check you receive from the utility company. Community gardens could pad the grocery budgets of households all over Shakopee.
  • Make Shakopee fun. We are known for Valley Fair, for the Renaissance Festival, for Canterbury Park and Mystic Lake. We have an amphitheater now, we have Rhythm on the Rails — but the music should not stop there. Minnesota is known for its music culture, and that's because there's always somewhere you can go to see a show. Along with culture festivals that celebrate the people who live here, I want the city's economic development tools working to develop Shakopee's cultural footprint. Let's make Shakopee known for more than just our biggest venues, where people go to spend the day and then leave without spending a single cent in Shakopee itself. Let's capitalize on the tourism those attractions bring by becoming the shining gem of a city that they're actually here to see.

Experience

  • ICE resistance organizer — countless hours this year protecting Shakopee families from ICE. Doing the work that I knew could get me killed, or get my freedom taken away, but that I knew had to be done simply because it was the right thing to do.
  • Shakopee Parks Board — a full term on the board that governs what my partner Jen and I love most: walking in Shakopee's beautiful parks. I was proud to lead a vote to keep Shakopee's athletic fields free for any team to use, as the alternative would have charged those teams a fee at no economic benefit to the city whatsoever.
  • Teamsters union steward — two years on the shop floor standing up for my coworkers when it counted. I had a great relationship with both management and the employees there, and I was well respected despite being in my early twenties, so making sure everyone was treated fairly only ever took a conversation.
  • Candidate for MN House 54B (2022) — I ran, and I listened at doors and kitchen tables across the district, and I learned what it means to represent a people in the political arena. Frankly, it made me want to stay out of politics for good. But when the community I helped protect, along with the people who helped protect it, all asked me to run, I knew it was the right thing to do.

The fine print — Minnesota's rules for donors
  • Personal funds only. Minnesota law prohibits campaign contributions from corporations and businesses — only give from a personal account, not a business one.
  • Contribution limit. You can give up to $600 total during 2026, per person.
  • If your total giving passes $100, state law requires the committee to report your name, address, and employer (or occupation, if self-employed) — so collecting that information is part of donating.
  • No anonymous donations. Every contribution needs a name attached so we can report honestly.
  • U.S. citizens and green-card holders only — federal law prohibits contributions from foreign nationals in any U.S. election, including local ones.
  • Not tax-deductible. The state's Political Contribution Refund program covers state races only, so it does not apply here. Please don't count on money back.
