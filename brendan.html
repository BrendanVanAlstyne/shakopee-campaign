Brendan Van Alstyne

for Shakopee City Council

Strength in Community

Why I'm Running

I didn't plan to run for office. When ICE was terrorizing our community this year, my fellow activists and I organized to protect our neighbors — because many of the people in power wouldn't. When it was over, those same activists asked me to run. Begged, really.

Here's what I took from that fight: we need people who have already proven they'll risk everything for this community sitting in the rooms where decisions get made. I believe in the power of human beings working together to overcome evil. That's not a slogan to me — it's what I watched my neighbors do this year.

I see the job of a city councilmember as simple: listen to the needs of this community, and give those needs a voice in city hall.

Experience

  • Shakopee Parks Board — served a full term working on the parks and public spaces that make this town worth living in.
  • Teamsters Union steward — several years standing up for coworkers on the shop floor.
  • Candidate for MN House 54B (2022) — I've done the work of listening to this district before.
  • Community organizer — countless hours this year protecting Shakopee families from ICE and from the fallout of its terror campaign.

Priorities

  • Protect this community. Build the local infrastructure — the policies, the networks, the preparedness — so Shakopee is never again caught flat-footed when the federal government comes after our neighbors.
  • Make life more affordable. Use every lever the city actually controls to lower the cost of living here. [SPECIFICS TO ADD — housing? fees? utilities?]
  • Make Shakopee fun! More live music, and not just at the amphitheater — our parks and our downtown should have it too. Culture festivals that celebrate the people who actually live here and the art they make. And I want the city's economic development tools and permitting to work for immigrant-owned businesses trying to open their doors — a Vietnamese grocer, a Cameroonian clothing shop, cultural goods you currently have to leave town to buy. A town where people want to be is a town that takes care of itself.
