Why I'm Running

I've loved this city ever since my family moved here in 2014. But never have I loved it so much as I did this past winter. When ICE came for our neighbors, nobody asked whether it was their job to do something. We just started doing whatever we could — anything that might help in some small way. We organized an active network of rapid responders, and we did our absolute best to make sure that nobody in our city could be brutalized or disappeared by those thugs.

When we faced escalation after escalation, and the murder of two citizens who were fighting for justice the same way we were, we didn't back down. We gritted our teeth and kept up the good work, knowing every day very well could be our last — praying every night that no ICE agent would meet the same violence they were meeting us with, because we were so afraid of what more unlawful and unhinged evils the government would do to us if only they could come up with an excuse.

The fight isn't over. ICE still operates in our city, just a little more quietly now. We are still here, doing all we can to keep our community safe. When I talk to my neighbors, when I ask them about their experience during the metro surge, I am often met with a fear to speak up — because they feel they might be met with retaliation from the government.

The people who will risk everything to use what little power they have to protect this community are the ones we should be electing as our leaders. I believe in the power of human beings working together to overcome evil. Strength in Community is more than just a slogan to me. It is what we found this year when we came together to give it our all against a threat too big for any one of us.

Compared with that work, the job of a councilmember is simple. I promise to listen to the needs of this community, and to give them a voice in city hall.

The fine print — Minnesota's rules for donors
  • Personal funds only. Minnesota law prohibits campaign contributions from corporations and businesses — only give from a personal account, not a business one.
  • Contribution limit. You can give up to $600 total during 2026, per person.
  • If your total giving passes $100, state law requires the committee to report your name, address, and employer (or occupation, if self-employed) — so collecting that information is part of donating.
  • No anonymous donations. Every contribution needs a name attached so we can report honestly.
  • U.S. citizens and green-card holders only — federal law prohibits contributions from foreign nationals in any U.S. election, including local ones.
  • Not tax-deductible. The state's Political Contribution Refund program covers state races only, so it does not apply here. Please don't count on money back.
