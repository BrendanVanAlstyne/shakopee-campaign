Why I'm Running

Back in December, when ICE came, I joined the fight to protect my neighbors without a second thought. I have been connected to and working with this community ever since.

Two council seats are up for election this year, and I didn't feel the registered candidates had a connection with — or cared enough for — the people of Shakopee. The community felt the same, and asked if anyone would step up and run.

So I did.

I care deeply for this city and its people. I want the people here to truly thrive. Sitting on city council, I would do whatever I can to help make that happen.

Priorities

  • Cost of living. A chief complaint I hear from people is about property taxes and rent — and gas and grocery prices are high. We need to balance the budget to prioritize affordability. I also believe in investing in green energy, local businesses, and building community to alleviate costs and help people thrive.
  • Green initiative. Summer heat puts strain on our electrical grid, and data centers only exacerbate the demands. Some areas have already been dealing with power issues — I don't want to see that here. We should get ahead of the issue and invest in solar and wind power. That gives us self-sufficiency and reduces utility costs.
  • Make Shakopee a good place to live, work, and play. Shakopee has a lot of cool stuff to bring in people from out of town. But when the show's over — when Ren Fest is done — they go home. I want to see more art, history, cultural celebrations, and music, and not just when the weather is nice! We would benefit from a decent indoor music venue, and a museum or two: things for the people who live here to enjoy that would also entice visitors to stay (and spend more money at our local businesses!).
  • Community building. I have seen the strength and beauty of community coming together, and I want to continue to see it grow. Community gardening, volunteer work, mutual aid — there is so much we can do, and we already know how to look out for each other. Let's keep it up and build a better Shakopee together.

Experience

  • Union strong — I have a long history of taking people's grievances and turning them into real change. I started doing this in school, and turned it into an active role as a contract negotiator. I have even lobbied for my union — and won some major changes.
  • Community involvement — There has never been a time I haven't been involved in something: Minnesota Hungarian since childhood, volunteering at Open Arms and Ozarks Food Harvest, yearly volunteering at St. John's in Savage, ICE Watch, and my current community work.
  • Education — Before I decided I'd hate spending 40 hours a week in front of a computer doing bookwork, I completed several college classes in accounting, mathematics, and business and management. My high GPA put me on the Dean's List.

Support the Campaign

The fine print — Minnesota's rules for donors
  • Personal funds only. Minnesota law prohibits campaign contributions from corporations and businesses — only give from a personal account, not a business one.
  • Contribution limit. You can give up to $600 total during 2026, per person.
  • If your total giving passes $100, state law requires the committee to report your name, address, and employer (or occupation, if self-employed) — so collecting that information is part of donating.
  • No anonymous donations. Every contribution needs a name attached so we can report honestly.
  • U.S. citizens and green-card holders only — federal law prohibits contributions from foreign nationals in any U.S. election, including local ones.
  • Not tax-deductible. The state's Political Contribution Refund program covers state races only, so it does not apply here. Please don't count on money back.
